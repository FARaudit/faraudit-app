# You're About to Find Out Why You Lost

Under FAR Case 2026-001, if you file an agency-level protest and elect independent review above the contracting officer, the agency would be required to give you two documents: a redacted copy of its final technical evaluation of your proposal, and a redacted copy of the source selection decision. You'd also be allowed to raise additional protest grounds within a reasonable time set by the reviewing official.

The rule states this disclosure would be available for any type of procurement.

I want to be careful about how much weight I put on that, so let me be specific about what I actually know.

**What those documents contain**

I spent eight years inside primes on the program-finance side. I've seen source selection decision documents and technical evaluations from the winning side of the table — the version that gets written when the award goes your way, and the internal reconstruction when it doesn't.

They are not summaries. A technical evaluation is where the government writes down, factor by factor, what it thought your proposal actually said and where it fell short. A source selection decision is where it explains the tradeoff it made and why. Between them, they contain the answer to the only question that matters after a loss: was this price, or was this us?

Right now, a small sub who loses almost never sees either. You get a debriefing if you're entitled to one, and debriefings vary enormously in usefulness. Everything else is inference.

**Why the forum matters more than the disclosure**

The rule puts a number on something most subs have felt: agency protests resolve in 35 days, GAO protests in 100 or more.

That gap is why the protest route is functionally closed to small companies. GAO means outside counsel, months of your attention, and a relationship cost with a customer you want to sell to for the next decade. For most subs the calculation ends before it starts — you eat the loss and move on.

An agency protest that resolves in about five weeks and comes with the evaluation record attached is a different instrument. The rule's own reasoning is that most protests are filed with limited information, and that insight into the evaluation and award decision replaces guesswork with facts — which the Council expects will sometimes make protests moot or cause them to be withdrawn.

Read that from the sub's side: you might file, get the documents, learn you lost on price, and drop it. You've spent five weeks and gained the one thing nobody gives you — a real answer, in writing.

**The other edge**

This isn't a gift to protesters. The same rule adds a purpose statement for the protest system explicitly intended to deter abuse and discourage frivolous protests, and it requires contracting officers to report protests to the head of the contracting activity. More management visibility on protests cuts in both directions — it surfaces agency-level problems, and it makes your protest visible to people above the CO you're trying to build a relationship with.

**Where my experience runs out**

I've never sat in the seat where you decide whether to protest. I know what those documents contain; I don't know what it costs a 40-person shop to file, relationally or in hours, or how that math changes when disclosure is part of the deal.

If you've made that call: does an evaluation record change it, or is the relationship risk the whole story regardless of what you'd learn?

The comment window closed July 23. What's in the docket now is what the final rule responds to.
